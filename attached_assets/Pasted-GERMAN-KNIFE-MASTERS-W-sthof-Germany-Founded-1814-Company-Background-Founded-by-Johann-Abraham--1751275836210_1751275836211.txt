GERMAN KNIFE MASTERS
Wüsthof - Germany (Founded 1814)
Company Background: Founded by Johann Abraham Wüsthof in 1814 in Solingen, Germany’s “City of Blades,” Wüsthof began as a small water-powered grinding workshop producing scissors. The company has remained family-owned for over 200 years and seven generations, with the famous trident trademark registered in 1895. Wüsthof pioneered the Classic forged knife series in the 1970s and is now one of the world’s largest manufacturers of chef’s knives.

Henckels (Zwilling) - Germany (Founded 1731)
Company Background: Founded on June 13, 1731, by Peter Henckels in Solingen, making it one of the oldest operating companies in the world. The iconic twin logo was registered with the Cutlers’ Guild of Solingen in 1731, making it one of the earliest trademarked companies. Named after Johann Abraham Henckels (1771-1850), the company has been owned by Werhahn KG since 1970.

F. Dick - Germany (Founded 1778)
Company Background: Founded by Johann Friedrich Dick in 1778 in Esslingen as a file manufacturer. The company transitioned to sharpening steels in 1873 under Paul Friedrich Dick’s leadership, then expanded to knives for butchers and chefs in 1889. Known for their arrow trademark and regarded as market leaders in butcher’s tools across Europe and North America.

FRENCH ARTISAN KNIVES
Sabatier - France (Founded 1810)
Company Background: Founded by Philippe Sabatier in 1810 when he purchased the “K” brand in Thiers, France’s knife-making capital. A highly trained scissor maker, Sabatier applied his metallurgy expertise to create precise cutting tools that became synonymous with French cuisine. The K-Sabatier brand remains with Philippe’s family descendants over 200 years later.

Opinel - France (Founded 1890)
Company Background: Founded by Joseph Opinel in 1890 in Savoie, France, as a working man’s knife for local farmers and herdsmen. The iconic wooden-handled design with the “crowned hand” trademark was chosen in 1909. The famous Virobloc safety locking mechanism was invented by Marcel Opinel in 1955. The company now sells approximately 15 million knives annually worldwide.

Laguiole - France (Founded 1829)
Company Background: First appeared around 1829 in the village of Laguiole, Aveyron region, created by blacksmith Pierre-Jean Calmels. Originally inspired by the Spanish navaja brought by seasonal workers, the knife became famous for its distinctive bee decoration and association with shepherds and café culture. The name “Laguiole” is not legally protected, leading to many imitations worldwide.

SCANDINAVIAN CRAFTSMANSHIP
Helle - Norway (Founded 1932)
Company Background: Established in 1932 by brothers Steinar and Sigmund Helle (aged 28 and 21) in Holmedal, Norway. They learned smithing from their village blacksmith father and started in the old family farm’s blacksmith shop. The company began without electricity, using a small dam and turbine for power, operating only when it rained. Now employs about 50 people and is Norway’s leading knife manufacturer.

Mora Knives - Sweden (Various Origins)
Company Background: Mora of Sweden was formed in 2005 through the merger of Frosts Knivfabrik (founded 1891 by Erik Frost) and KJ Eriksson (founded 1912). The town of Östnor, Mora has been a knife-making center for centuries, with local craftsmen passing down skills generationally. Today produces almost 4 million knives annually and holds a Royal Warrant from the King of Sweden since 2011.

SWISS PRECISION
Victorinox - Switzerland (Founded 1884)
Company Background: Founded in 1884 by Karl Elsener as “Messerfabrik Carl Elsener” in Ibach, Switzerland. Named “Victoria” in 1909 after Elsener’s mother, then became “Victorinox” in 1921 (combining “Victoria” and “inox” for stainless steel). Famous for creating the Swiss Army Knife, the company now produces over 60,000 pocket knives daily and reached 500 million Swiss Army Knives produced by 2017.

SPANISH HERITAGE
Pallarés Solsona - Spain (Founded 1917)
Company Background: Founded in 1917 by brothers Lluís and Carles Pallarès Canal in Solsona, Spain. Solsona’s knife-making tradition dates back to the 16th century, once having 24 workshops in the Guild of Saint Eligius. Today, Pallarés is the sole surviving testimony to Solsona’s renowned knife-making industry, now run by the third generation while maintaining hand-sharpening traditions.

JAPANESE PRECISION
MAC Knives - Japan (Founded 1965)
Company Background: Founded in 1965 by Tatsuo Kobayashi after an accidental discovery in 1954 while working as a chef in Chicago. When Kobayashi accidentally broke the tip of a German knife and reshaped it with a round point, he discovered improved usability. The name “MAC” comes from “MACROCOSMOS,” reflecting Kobayashi’s ambition to reach global markets. Over 30 million MAC knives have been sold worldwide.

Okatsune - Japan (Founded 1940s)
Company Background: Founded in the 1940s by Tsuneichi Okano in Hiroshima, who began as a blacksmith apprentice in 1925. The name “Okatsune” derives from Okano’s own name. The company forges tools from Izumo Yasugi steel, traditionally used for Samurai Katana swords, achieving unrivaled Rockwell hardness of 60.0-61.0. The distinctive red and white handles symbolize happiness in Japanese culture.
