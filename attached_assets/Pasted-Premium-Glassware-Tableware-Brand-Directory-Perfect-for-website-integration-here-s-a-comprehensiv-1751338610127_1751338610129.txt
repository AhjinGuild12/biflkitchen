FRENCH GLASS INNOVATION
Duralex - France (Founded 1945)
Company Background: Founded in 1945 in La Chapelle-Saint-Mesmin, France, near Orléans. Revolutionary inventors of the glass tempering process, heating molded glass to 600°C then cooling rapidly for 2.5x impact resistance. Famous for the iconic Picardie tumbler (1954) and Gigogne glass, with the latter earning a place in the permanent collection of Paris Musée des Arts Décoratifs. The brand name derives from the Latin “dura lex, sed lex” meaning “the law is harsh, but it is the law”.

FINNISH DESIGN EXCELLENCE
Fiskars - Finland (Founded 1649)
Company Background: Founded in 1649 in Fiskars village, Finland, by Dutch merchant Peter Thorwöste as an ironworks. One of the world’s oldest continuously operating companies, transitioning from iron to copper processing in the 18th century, then to cutlery production with Finland’s first cutlery mill in 1832. Famous worldwide for their distinctive orange-handled scissors introduced in 1967. Listed on Helsinki Stock Exchange since 1915.

Iittala - Finland (Founded 1881)
Company Background: Founded in 1881 by Swedish glassworker Peter Magnus Abrahamson in Iittala village, Finland. Started with 17 Swedish glassblowers producing the first glass objects in November 1881. Famous for collaborations with renowned designers like Alvar Aalto (Savoy Vase, 1936), Aino Aalto, and Oiva Toikka. Now part of Fiskars Group, continues traditional Finnish glassmaking with modern design innovation.

GERMAN PRECISION GLASS
Schott Zwiesel - Germany (Founded 1884)
Company Background: Founded in 1884 as a glass laboratory in Jena, Germany, by glass chemist Otto Schott (1851-1935), physicist Ernst Abbe (1840-1905), and optician Carl Zeiss (1816-1888). Started as the Schott & Associates Glass Technology Laboratory for developing new types of specialty glass. The company acquired the glassworks in Zwiesel in 1927, marking the beginnings of the SCHOTT Group. Transformed from a small glass laboratory into one of the world’s leading manufacturers of specialty glass through continuous innovation.

ITALIAN ARTISAN GLASS
Bormioli Rocco - Italy (Founded 1825)
Company Background: Founded in 1825 when Luigi Bormioli left Altare (Province of Savona) and moved to Borgo San Donnino (now Fidenza) in the province of Parma. The Bormioli family glass-making tradition dates back to AD 1300 in Altare. After Luigi’s death in 1832, his wife Petronilla led the company for 22 years with their three sons. Became the Bormioli Rocco Glass and Son in 1880. By World War I, employed over 1,600 people with a worldwide sales network. Today operates nine plants, two decorative ateliers, and nine stores with presence in over 100 countries.

SWEDISH PORCELAIN HERITAGE
Rörstrand - Sweden (Founded 1726)
Company Background: Founded in 1726 when porcelain production began at Rörstrand Castle in Stockholm. Named “Rörstrand” because the clear lake’s shore was overgrown with reeds. Was the only major Swedish porcelain factory until 1785 and the only porcelain manufacturer in Sweden until the late 1750s. During the 1860s, became one of the nation’s largest factories. By 1900, employed around 1,100 people with an excellent worldwide reputation. Production moved from Stockholm to Gothenburg in 1926, then to Lidköping in the 1930s. Celebrated 250 years in 1976 with the opening of the Rörstrand Museum holding over 15,000 pieces.
